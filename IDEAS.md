# Ideas for later

Not built yet. Most of these teach that **profit isn't cash**, and most add a new kind of line to the books. Keep on-screen text short and don't tell players what they can figure out themselves.

## Getting paid
- **Cash is a pain.** People pay with $20s, so you need a morning float of $1s and coins (a trip to the bank). Run out of change and you lose the sale. The cash count sometimes doesn't match the register tape. Carrying lots of cash is a risk; deposits take time.
- **Card readers.** A monthly rental plus about 3% of each sale, but people with no cash can buy, and they spend a bit more. The money arrives two days later: a new asset, "Money the card company owes us". Later: chargebacks, "card minimum $5".
- **Unused gift cards.** Some never come back. At the end of the summer, decide whether the money you're holding for them is now yours.
- **Prepaid party bookings.** A deposit now for ice cream later (like gift cards), with refund questions if they cancel.
- **Bills sent to customers.** The library or the town fair pays 30 days later, and some pay late. A big sale can still leave you short of cash.

## Costs that don't feel like costs
- **The truck wears out** (depreciation): an expense with no cash leaving.
- **Repair or upgrade?** A flat tire is an expense; a new freezer is something you own.
- **Insurance and permits** paid up front: an asset that turns into an expense week by week.
- **Sales tax.** Collected for the town; it sits in the cash box but isn't yours.

## Supplies and planning
- **Order ahead.** Supplies arrive the next day or next week, and fast shipping costs extra. Adds "Supplies on the way" (paid for, not here yet).
- **Automatic reordering.** Pick the pack size and how low stock gets before reordering. Fits best once there are employees: the rules are the instructions you leave them.
- **Weather forecast** for the next few days, sometimes wrong (more often the further out it looks), so you buy less mix that spoils before a rainy week. Most useful together with ordering ahead.
- **Will I have enough cash on Tuesday?** A simple forecast before a big order or event.
- **A competitor truck** parks at your best spot.
- **Suppliers raise prices** mid-summer, unless you signed a contract earlier.

## Trucks and starting out
- **Cheap used trucks** on the lot: shabby looks mean fewer sales in the fancy neighborhoods until it's repainted, plus worse gas mileage and breakdowns (a lost day and a repair bill).
- **A push cart** selling popsicles: no truck, no gas, a small area and no bank loan. Borrow from a relative instead: maybe no interest, but a promise to pay back. Could be an easier first summer before graduating to a truck.

## Growing (summer 2 and later)
- **Paying for a second truck:** another loan (interest), selling part of the business to a grandparent (a share of the profit forever), or leasing.
- **Employees:** wages, a schedule, a tip jar (whose money is it?), and their own register tapes.
- **Fraud,** caught with the books already in the game:
  - Skimming: the inventory of supplies used says 300 cones, the register tape says 250 sold.
  - Fake bills from a supplier nobody ordered from, easy to miss in papers handed to Penny.
  - Padded gas receipts.
  - Ways to stop it: whoever counts the cash doesn't file, surprise counts, comparing weeks.
- **Money laundering, gently:** a "friend" pays you to report extra sales. The clue is sales way beyond what your supplies could make.
- **Grandma's been laundering money.** The clue is in the books: her investments or "gifts" don't add up. Do you blow the whistle on Grandma, even though she's helped you all along?
- **The bank reads your books** at loan time: clean books mean a cheaper loan.

## Suggested order

3. Sales tax.
4. Order ahead plus the weather forecast.
